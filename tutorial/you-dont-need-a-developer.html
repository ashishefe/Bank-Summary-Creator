You Don't Need a Developer

The twist: your CA expertise is the hard part. The coding is the easy part now.

What Is Claude Code?

Think of a senior software developer sitting next to you. You describe what you want in plain English — or Hindi, or Hinglish — and they write the code, test it, fix bugs, and explain what they did. You don't need to understand the code. You need to understand the problem.

That's Claude Code. It's a tool from Anthropic that runs in your terminal — the black window where you type commands. You describe what you want, and it writes working software.

Why Domain Expertise Beats Coding Skill

Most people get this wrong about building software: the hard part isn't writing code. The hard part is knowing what to build.

A developer who doesn't understand CA work would need weeks just to learn what 26AS reconciliation means, why the financial year runs April to March, what Section 194A implies, or why opening balance matters in bank statement parsing. They'd build something that sort of works but misses all the edge cases you deal with every day.

You already know all of this. You know that banks format PDFs differently. You know what a TDS mismatch looks like. You know that cash deposits over ₹10 lakh trigger SFT reporting. You know what questions to ask a client when their AIS doesn't match their bank statements.

The real equation

Your domain knowledge (what to build, what edge cases matter, what the output should look like) + Claude Code (writes the actual code) = software that actually works for CAs.

What "Building with AI" Actually Looks Like

A realistic example of how a conversation with Claude Code goes:

You: I want to parse ICICI bank statement PDFs. The format has Date, Description, Debit, Credit, Balance columns. Dates are in DD/MM/YYYY format. Some descriptions span two lines. Claude: I'll create an ICICI parser. Let me look at the existing parser structure first... [reads files, writes code, adds tests] Done. The parser handles multi-line descriptions and extracts all 5 columns. Want me to run the tests? You: Yes, run the tests. Also, sometimes the opening balance row doesn't have a date — handle that too. Claude: Good catch. I've updated the parser to detect opening balance rows without dates. All 12 tests pass.

Notice what happened: you described the problem domain (ICICI format, date format, multi-line quirk). Claude wrote the code. You caught an edge case from your experience. Claude fixed it. This is the workflow.

Low Stakes, High Upside

This is a hobby project. No deadlines, and if something breaks, Claude will fix it. Worst case, you learn something. Best case, you build a tool that materially changes how your office handles tax season.
